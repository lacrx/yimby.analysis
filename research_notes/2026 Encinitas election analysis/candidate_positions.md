# 2026 Encinitas Election Candidate Analysis

**Note on races:** The user's original race pairings were incorrect. The actual matchups, confirmed across multiple sources, are:

- **Mayor (Short Term, 2-year):** Bruce Ehlers (incumbent) vs. Rimga Viskanta
- **District 3 (open seat, Joy Lyndes retiring):** Arielle Golden vs. Ed Sprague
- **District 4:** Brad Lefkowits vs. Marco San Antonio (appointed incumbent)

Dane White vs. Elias Velazquez is the **Escondido** mayoral race, not Encinitas.

---

## Faction Alignment Summary

### Takeaway

Encinitas politics since 2022 have been defined by a factional split between a conservative/anti-density council majority (Ehlers, O'Hara, Shaffer, San Antonio) and progressives aligned with former Mayor/now State Sen. Catherine Blakespear. The 2026 races pit continuation candidates for each faction against each other. The Ehlers-aligned candidates are Ehlers himself, Ed Sprague, and Marco San Antonio. The progressive/Blakespear-aligned candidates are Rimga Viskanta, Arielle Golden, and Brad Lefkowits.

### Cited Findings

- The current council includes one Democrat (Joy Lyndes), one Republican, and three Independents whose policies and rhetoric often lean conservative. -- [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2026/09/23/two-opposite-cities-one-similar-battle-for-mayor/)
- San Diego County Democratic Party endorses: Viskanta (Mayor), Golden (D3), Lefkowits (D4). -- [KPBS Endorsement Guide](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/09/30/2026-general-election-guide-to-endorsements-from-san-diego-democrats-republicans-green-libertarian)
- San Diego & Imperial Counties Labor Council endorses: Golden (D3), Lefkowits (D4). -- [Union Yes 2026 Endorsements](https://www.unionyes.org/2026-endorsements)
- ENCDC (Encinitas Democratic Club) endorses: Golden (D3), Lefkowits (D4). -- [ENCDC Endorsements](https://www.encdc.org/endorsements)
- Conserve North County (progressive PAC) endorses Arielle Golden. -- [Conserve North County](https://www.conservenorthcounty.org/)
- Ed Sprague received a 2020 $400 payment to the Republican Party of San Diego for mailer inclusion. He is endorsed by Carl DeMaio's Reform Party. -- [Fear and Loathing in Encinitas](https://fearandloathinginencinitas.substack.com/p/encinitas-election-2026)
- Marco San Antonio is endorsed by Carl DeMaio's Reform Party and grassroots conservative organizations. -- [KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/09/30/2026-general-election-guide-to-endorsements-from-san-diego-democrats-republicans-green-libertarian)
- Viskanta is endorsed by Rep. Mike Levin (D-CA49) and Assemblymember Tasha Boerner (D-AD77). -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- The Fear and Loathing in Encinitas Substack labels the Ehlers council majority as "MAGA-aligned," though this is editorial characterization, not a self-description. -- [Fear and Loathing in Encinitas](https://fearandloathinginencinitas.substack.com/p/encinitas-election-2026)

### Inferences

- The endorsement pattern is clean: Democratic Party, labor, and progressive PACs line up behind Viskanta/Golden/Lefkowits. DeMaio's Reform Party and conservative groups line up behind Ehlers/Sprague/San Antonio.
- The Blakespear connection is indirect but real: Blakespear's state bill (SB 569 / trailer bill language) to protect the Santa Fe bike lane put her in direct opposition to the Ehlers majority. Viskanta, while she criticized Blakespear's legislative method, shares the progressive coalition's endorsers and opposes Ehlers' approach to governance.

## Mayor: Bruce Ehlers (Incumbent) vs. Rimga Viskanta

### Positions on Housing Development and Density

#### Takeaway

Ehlers is the most explicitly anti-density candidate in any race. He authored Proposition A (2013), which requires voter approval for zoning density/height increases, and his top campaign priority is "defending against overdevelopment." Viskanta takes a moderate reform position: she criticizes state mandates but wants collaborative reform rather than litigation, and she favors converting existing units to affordable housing and strengthening inclusionary requirements.

#### Cited Findings

**Ehlers:**
- Authored Proposition A (2013), giving Encinitas voters the right to decide on zoning density and height increases. -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- Campaign top priority: "defend against overdevelopment" and "restore local control over zoning." -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- Website states he will "defend Encinitas against excessive development while complying with state affordable housing mandates." -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- He and the current council are backing a statewide ballot initiative to change state housing laws. -- [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2026/09/23/two-opposite-cities-one-similar-battle-for-mayor/)
- Served on the Planning Commission until councilmembers removed him for "supporting a court case against the city over its housing plans" (i.e., siding with Proposition A opponents of the housing element). -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)

**Viskanta:**
- "We cannot count on large-scale private developers to solve the affordable housing crisis." -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- Wants to "work with regional and state leaders to rethink and reform density bonus laws." Argues the state density bonus law's affordability threshold is too low. Prefers the city's 20% inclusionary zoning ordinance as a baseline. -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- Priorities include preserving 11 mobile home parks through rent-increase limits and working with nonprofit developers on 100% affordable projects. -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- "Prefer to work collaboratively with legislators and other partners to reform state housing law." -- [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2026/09/23/two-opposite-cities-one-similar-battle-for-mayor/)
- She disagrees with state housing policy but opposes the incumbent's "fight the state" strategy. -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)

#### Inferences

- On the YIMBY/Strong Towns scale, neither candidate is truly pro-housing-supply. Ehlers is clearly anti-density (-2 territory: density caps, opposing state mandates, Prop A author). Viskanta is more nuanced but still frames housing through an anti-market-rate lens ("cannot count on large-scale private developers") and wants to strengthen inclusionary requirements, which scores as moderate (+1 tenant protections, mobile home park preservation) but includes supply-skeptical elements.
- Viskanta is the clearly preferable candidate from a pro-urbanist perspective, but she is not a YIMBY champion. She is a pragmatic moderate who would shift the city from active obstruction to grudging compliance with reform ambitions.

### Positions on Bike Lanes and Transportation

#### Takeaway

Ehlers is the leader of the bikelash movement in Encinitas. He led the effort to remove the $4.15M protected bike lane on Santa Fe Drive and the lane in front of San Dieguito Academy. Viskanta supported staff-recommended changes to Santa Fe (keeping the protected infrastructure) and frames her transportation policy around child safety and "tomorrow's mobility choices."

#### Cited Findings

**Ehlers:**
- Led the effort to remove the protected bike lane in front of San Dieguito Academy, citing emergency response times, parking, and design flaws. -- [KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/public-safety/2026/03/02/encinitas-to-remove-protected-bike-lane-in-front-of-san-dieguito-academy)
- Has said "protected bike lanes are not necessarily safer than painted lanes" and that "cars and bikes should share space where lanes conflict with turns." -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- Called Blakespear's bike lane protection bill "too prescriptive in nature" and said it "will not stop us from making Santa Fe or the 101 safer." -- [Multiple sources on SB 569](https://sd.streetsblog.org/2026/09/08/california-legislature-moves-to-stop-encinitas-from-tearing-out-state-funded-santa-fe-bike-lanes/)
- Cited a post-installation survey: "It was universally hated, 90% of the respondents did not like it." -- [NBC San Diego](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/local/encinitas-city-council-votes-redo-bike-paths-santa-fe-drive-corridor/4041313/)
- The Santa Fe Drive protected lanes were completed in 2025 for $4.15 million in state funds. The council voted 4-1 in November 2025 to remove them. -- [NBC San Diego](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/local/encinitas-city-council-votes-redo-bike-paths-santa-fe-drive-corridor/4041313/)
- Prioritizes "pavement, storm drains, and Leucadia Boulevard flooding over vanity and beautification projects." -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)

**Viskanta:**
- Supported staff-recommended changes to the Santa Fe Drive bike lane (keeping the protected infrastructure), which the council rejected when it voted to remove the protected lane. -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- Wants to "deliver safer streets designed to protect children" and neighbors who cannot drive. -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- Says Encinitas should not be a "cut-through corridor" for drivers reaching the freeway. -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- Plans for "tomorrow's mobility choices," including e-bikes and driverless cars. -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- Supports the red light camera pilot program. -- [KPBS Mayoral Explainer](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- Criticizes the council for overreach on traffic projects: "I think the current climate in the city council chambers is one where they are designing traffic projects from the dais." and "I don't think they're respecting the expertise of staff." -- [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2026/09/23/two-opposite-cities-one-similar-battle-for-mayor/)

#### Inferences

- Ehlers is unambiguously bikelash-aligned. He removed two protected bike lanes, dismisses protected infrastructure as unnecessary, and frames bike safety improvements as "vanity projects."
- Viskanta is pro-safe-streets and pro-bike-infrastructure, though she frames it through child safety rather than urbanist ideology. Her criticism of the council "designing traffic projects from the dais" instead of deferring to staff is a strong signal that she would restore professional traffic engineering judgment over political bike-lane removal.

## District 3: Arielle Golden vs. Ed Sprague (Open Seat)

### Positions on Housing Development and Density

#### Takeaway

Golden takes a moderate pro-housing stance focused on workforce housing and criticizing the city's confrontational approach to state law, while Sprague aligns with the Ehlers faction's local control and anti-urbanization positions. - The "all candidates supported requiring two parking spots per unit for new housing" finding from the forum is significant: even the progressive candidates did not challenge parking minimums.

## The Bike Lane Controversy: Context

### Takeaway

The Santa Fe Drive bike lane is the defining bikelash battle in North County. The Ehlers council removed $4.15M in state-funded protected bike infrastructure, prompting Sen. Blakespear to pursue legislation specifically blocking the removal. This became the central culture-war issue in the mayoral race.

### Cited Findings

- The city declared a local emergency over bike safety in 2023 after multiple serious and fatal crashes. -- [NBC San Diego](https://nbcsandiego.com/news/local/after-teen-killed-in-e-bike-accident-encinitas-gets-3m-in-safety-improvements/3311378)
- The Santa Fe Drive protected lanes were completed in 2025 for $4.15 million in state funds. -- [Streetsblog](https://sd.streetsblog.org/2026/09/08/california-legislature-moves-to-stop-encinitas-from-tearing-out-state-funded-santa-fe-bike-lanes/)
- The council voted 4-1 in November 2025 to remove contested features along Santa Fe Drive. Councilmember Joy Lyndes cast the lone dissenting vote, arguing most attendees at the meeting favored keeping the protected lane. -- [NBC San Diego](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/local/encinitas-city-council-votes-redo-bike-paths-santa-fe-drive-corridor/4041313/)
- In March 2026, the council voted to remove the protected bike lane in front of San Dieguito Academy High School. -- [KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/public-safety/2026/03/02/encinitas-to-remove-protected-bike-lane-in-front-of-san-dieguito-academy)
- Sen. Blakespear's SB 569 was defeated in the Assembly. She then pursued trailer bill language specifically prohibiting Encinitas from removing or modifying the Santa Fe Drive protected lane for five years. -- [Streetsblog](https://sd.streetsblog.org/2026/09/08/california-legislature-moves-to-stop-encinitas-from-tearing-out-state-funded-santa-fe-bike-lanes/); [Coast News](https://thecoastnews.com/blakespears-amended-bike-infrastructure-bill-divides-locals-advocates/)
- The Encinitas City Council was set to request a line-item veto from Governor Newsom on the Santa Fe provision at its Sept. 9 meeting. -- [Multiple sources]
- Bike advocacy group Encinitas Bike Walk's Marley Jaoudi: a bike lane helps "reduce the barrier to entry of biking safely around Encinitas." -- [Multiple sources]
- Deputy Mayor Jim O'Hara called Blakespear's bill "a last-ditch effort" to preserve the Santa Fe Drive configuration. -- [Multiple sources]

### Gaps
