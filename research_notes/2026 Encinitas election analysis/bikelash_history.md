# Encinitas Bikelash Political History (2018-2026)

## What specific bike infrastructure projects triggered the backlash?

### Takeaway
Two major projects catalyzed the bikelash: the Leucadia Streetscape on Highway 101 (roundabouts, lane reductions, bike lanes, and sidewalks along a 2.2-mile corridor) and the Santa Fe Drive Corridor Improvements (Class IV protected bike lanes, reverse-angle parking, lane narrowing). The Leucadia Streetscape was the slow-burn controversy during Blakespear's tenure; Santa Fe Drive became the acute flashpoint under the Ehlers council.

### Cited Findings
- The Leucadia Streetscape project was discussed as early as 1995 and approved by council in March 2018. It added up to six roundabouts, a single traffic lane in each direction on Highway 101, bike lanes, and sidewalks. The city's capital budget listed the Streetscape and 101 drainage work at $53M. — [San Diego Reader](https://sandiegoreader.com/news/2018/mar/30/encinitas-roundabout-capital-california); [Coast News](https://thecoastnews.com/encinitas-agrees-to-debt-financing-three-roundabouts-for-streetscape/)
- In April 2020, more than 100 residents protested outside Blakespear's home over COVID-era closures, including the Coastal Rail Trail. Critics feared the trail would restrict beach access and worsen traffic. — [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2026/01/08/encinitas-still-blames-former-mayor-catherine-blakespear-for-many-of-its-troubles/)
- Critics argued the city spent over $20 million on surface upgrades (roundabouts, bike lanes, sidewalks) while underground drainage fixes in Leucadia remained underfunded. Mayor Ehlers criticized the "beautification projects." — [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2026/01/08/encinitas-still-blames-former-mayor-catherine-blakespear-for-many-of-its-troubles/)
- The Leucadia Streetscape construction lasted over two years, with businesses along a roughly one-mile stretch complaining it was driving away customers. — [NBC San Diego](https://nbcsandiego.com/news/local/leucadia-businesses-say-highway-101-construction-is-driving-customers-away/4059400)
- The Santa Fe Drive Corridor project cost $4.15 million. Sen. Blakespear secured $3.09 million in state funds in July 2023 for construction. The design reduced lanes to 9 feet, added reverse-angled parking, and built Class IV protected bike lanes separated from parking by elevated landscaping. — [KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/09/10/the-encinitas-city-council-wants-to-remove-a-protected-bike-lane-a-state-senator-is-trying-to-save-it); [NC Pipeline](https://ncpipeline.substack.com/p/blakespears-bike-bill-ignites-encinitas-feud)
- Problems with Santa Fe Drive included: a combined bike lane/sidewalk that left only inches between the sidewalk and travel lane west of Nardo/Mackinnon; the U.S. Post Office stopped delivering mail for months before installing a cluster box in fall 2025; the city fired the contractor in December 2024 over shoddy work; and residents cited fire/ambulance access issues and seven blind right-hook turns into businesses. — [NC Pipeline](https://ncpipeline.substack.com/p/blakespears-bike-bill-ignites-encinitas-feud)
- One city commission member presented data showing a more than 400% increase in serious crashes compared to the 14 years before the installation of protected bike lanes on Santa Fe Drive. — [CBS 8](https://www.cbs8.com/article/news/local/encinitas-to-scale-back-4m-bike-lane-project-after-complaints/509-40996632-f4cf-4532-a631-56c90829ded4)
- In February 2026, the council voted to remove the protected lane in front of San Dieguito Academy High School. In March, the council approved a redesign switching from Class IV protected lanes to Class II painted lanes, replacing reverse-angled parking with parallel spots, and widening lanes to 11 feet. The redesign is expected to cost $3.4 to $3.6 million. Joy Lyndes cast the lone dissenting vote. — [KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/09/10/the-encinitas-city-council-wants-to-remove-a-protected-bike-lane-a-state-senator-is-trying-to-save-it)

### Inferences
- The Leucadia Streetscape was a longer-running grievance that built anti-infrastructure sentiment over years; Santa Fe Drive was a more acute, implementation-driven controversy where specific design failures (mail delivery disruption, contractor firing, access problems) gave concrete ammunition to opponents beyond ideological opposition.
- The $3.09M state funding for Santa Fe Drive created a direct link between Blakespear's state role and the local project, making the controversy personal and intergovernmental.

### Gaps

The Leucadia Streetscape runs along Highway 101, not Leucadia Boulevard. It is possible the user is referring to the 101 Streetscape or conflating corridors.
- Final construction status of the Leucadia Streetscape as of 2026 was not confirmed.


## How did the 2022 and 2024 elections play out?

### Takeaway
The user's premise that Ehlers beat Kranz in 2022 is incorrect. The bikelash wave came in two stages: a partial shift in 2022 (Ehlers won a council seat, not the mayoralty), followed by a decisive sweep in 2024 when Ehlers beat Kranz for mayor and two new anti-mandate candidates won council seats, creating a 4-1 majority.

### Cited Findings

**2022 Election:**
- Catherine Blakespear departed the mayor's office to run for state Senate (SD-38), which she won. — [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2026/01/08/encinitas-still-blames-former-mayor-catherine-blakespear-for-many-of-its-troubles/)
- Tony Kranz won the mayoral race in a four-candidate field with approximately 48.18% (8,011 votes) in early returns. — [North Coast Current](https://www.northcoastcurrent.com/encinitas/2022/11/kranz-holds-lead-for-encinitas-mayor-district-3-close-cannabis-tax-winning/)
- Bruce Ehlers won the District 4 council seat. — [Coast News](https://thecoastnews.com/kranz-to-be-next-mayor-encinitas-ehlers-wins-district-4-seat/)
- Joy Lyndes won District 3 over Julie Thunder, leading approximately 55% to 45% in early returns. — [North Coast Current](https://www.northcoastcurrent.com/encinitas/2022/11/kranz-holds-lead-for-encinitas-mayor-district-3-close-cannabis-tax-winning/)
- District 1 was not on the 2022 ballot. D1 members were elected in November 2020. — [City of Encinitas](https://encinitasca.gov/Government/Municipal-Elections)

**2024 Election:**
- Bruce Ehlers defeated incumbent Mayor Tony Kranz, 52.32% to 47.68% (12,775 to 11,644 in early returns; later updated to approximately 52.4% to 47.6%, or 14,921 to 13,652). Kranz conceded on Friday, Nov. 8. — [Patch](https://patch.com/california/encinitas/encinitas-mayor-race-flips-ehlers-leads-slim-margin); [NBC San Diego](https://nbcsandiego.com/news/local/san-diego-countys-mayoral-races-oceanside-within-300-votes-encinitas-incumbent-concedes/3674420/)
- Luke Shaffer defeated appointed incumbent Allison Blackwell in District 1, 56.44% to 43.56% (4,353 to 3,359). Blackwell had been appointed to fill Kranz's seat when he was elected mayor in 2022. — [North Coast Current](https://www.northcoastcurrent.com/encinitas/2024/11/encinitas-appears-headed-toward-city-council-sea-change/)
- Jim O'Hara defeated Destiny Preston in District 2, 55.70% to 44.30%. — [North Coast Current](https://www.northcoastcurrent.com/encinitas/2024/11/encinitas-appears-headed-toward-city-council-sea-change/)
- Ehlers had been removed as Planning Commission chair in April 2022 over his opposition to the city's housing policies. — [North Coast Current](https://www.northcoastcurrent.com/encinitas/2024/11/encinitas-appears-headed-toward-city-council-sea-change/)

### Inferences
- The 2024 sweep, not 2022, was the decisive bikelash/anti-mandate wave. In 2022, the slow-growth faction gained one council seat (Ehlers in D4) but failed to take the mayoralty. In 2024, Ehlers, Shaffer, and O'Hara created a 4-1 council majority with Joy Lyndes as the lone progressive voice.
- Ehlers's firing from the Planning Commission in 2022 became a rallying narrative for his 2024 mayoral run.
- The margins were comfortable: Ehlers won by ~5 points, Shaffer by ~13 points, O'Hara by ~11 points. This was not a fluke; the anti-mandate sentiment was broadly shared in 2024 Encinitas.

### Gaps
- Who won District 4 after Ehlers vacated it for the mayor's race in 2024? Marco San Antonio appears to hold that seat based on the 2026 race (he's the incumbent being challenged by Lefkowits), but I could not find reporting on his initial election/appointment.
- Shaffer's specific policy positions on bikes/housing are thin in the sources reviewed.


## What did the Ehlers/bikelash council majority do once in power (2022-2026)?

### Takeaway
The Ehlers council pursued a multi-front anti-mandate agenda: removing the Santa Fe Drive protected bike lanes, endorsing the Our Neighborhood Voices statewide ballot initiative to constitutionally override state housing laws, lobbying Sacramento for local control, closing the safe parking program, and tightening homeless enforcement. However, when confronted with direct state enforcement threats, the council approved major housing projects it opposed in principle, including the 448-unit Quail Meadows development.

### Cited Findings

**Bike Infrastructure Rollbacks:**
- In February 2026, the council voted to remove the protected bike lane in front of San Dieguito Academy. In March, the council voted 4-1 (Lyndes dissenting) to redesign Santa Fe Drive from Class IV protected lanes to Class II painted lanes, replace reverse-angled parking with parallel, and widen lanes. Estimated cost: $3.4-3.6 million. — [KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/09/10/the-encinitas-city-council-wants-to-remove-a-protected-bike-lane-a-state-senator-is-trying-to-save-it)
- On September 9, 2026, the council voted 4-1 to ask Gov. Newsom to line-item veto the budget bill language blocking the Santa Fe Drive changes. — [KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/09/10/the-encinitas-city-council-wants-to-remove-a-protected-bike-lane-a-state-senator-is-trying-to-save-it)
- Ehlers argued buffered lanes let cars pull over for emergency vehicles and that protected lanes are not necessarily safer than painted lanes. — [KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/09/10/the-encinitas-city-council-wants-to-remove-a-protected-bike-lane-a-state-senator-is-trying-to-save-it)

**Anti-State Housing Mandates:**
- The council voted 4-1 to endorse the Our Neighborhood Voices ballot initiative, which would amend the California constitution to let local governments override conflicting state housing laws. Oceanside and San Marcos also endorsed it. — [NBC San Diego](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/local/encinitas-joins-neighborhood-voices-effort-to-overrule-state-development-rule/3830034/)
- Ehlers said the goal was to qualify the measure for the 2026 or 2028 ballot. As of late 2026, the initiative does not appear to have qualified for the November 2026 ballot (a Manifold prediction market resolved "No"; the June 25, 2026 qualification deadline has passed). — [Manifold Markets](https://manifold.markets/DavidFWatson/will-our-neighborhood-voices-qualif); [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2025/03/07/encinitas-is-banking-on-the-state-to-let-them-limit-housing-but-lawmakers-arent-interested/)
- The council's legislative priorities include protecting roadways under local control, and they added roadways as a land-use type to oppose state efforts to undermine that control. — [NC Pipeline](https://ncpipeline.substack.com/p/blakespears-bike-bill-ignites-encinitas-feud)

**Housing Approvals Under Duress:**
- In February 2025, the council voted 3-1 to approve the 448-unit Quail Meadows project (90 affordable units). Before the vote, HCD warned that rejection would violate state law and could lead to Housing Element decertification and the Builder's Remedy. Ehlers called it a "Sophie's choice." — [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2025/12/29/north-county-cities-are-conforming-to-state-housing-laws-but-working-to-change-them-behind-the-scenes/)
- State lawmakers showed no interest in loosening housing mandates. Sen. Blakespear said she sees no appetite in Sacramento for the changes Encinitas wants and perceives a push for stricter enforcement. Assemblymember Chris Ward warned courts would uphold state law. — [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2025/03/07/encinitas-is-banking-on-the-state-to-let-them-limit-housing-but-lawmakers-arent-interested/)

**Other Policy Actions:**
- The safe parking program for homeless residents, opened in 2020 with state grant funding, was closed at the end of 2025 after the council offered only six months of continued funding, which the nonprofit operator deemed unsustainable. — [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2026/01/08/encinitas-still-blames-former-mayor-catherine-blakespear-for-many-of-its-troubles/)
- The council added an enforcement component to the Homeless Action Plan, tightened camping rules, and contracted with the San Diego Rescue Mission. — [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2026/01/08/encinitas-still-blames-former-mayor-catherine-blakespear-for-many-of-its-troubles/)

### Inferences
- The Ehlers council's most consequential bike action (Santa Fe Drive removal) was blocked by Blakespear's state budget trailer bill provision, turning a local rollback into a state-vs-city confrontation.
- On housing, the council's "fight the state" rhetoric exceeded its actual policy reach. When HCD threatened decertification (Quail Meadows), the council approved the project. The Our Neighborhood Voices initiative failed to qualify for the ballot. State lawmakers dismissed the local-control argument.
- The pattern suggests the anti-mandate faction could signal opposition but could not deliver policy outcomes against state enforcement. This is the core tension: the base expected defiance, but the council complied when penalties loomed.

### Gaps
- Specific council votes on housing projects beyond Quail Meadows and Goodson are not fully documented in the sources reviewed.
- Whether the council attempted to modify the city's inclusionary housing ordinance or zoning code beyond endorsing the statewide initiative is unclear.

## How did Encinitas handle housing element compliance under the new council?

### Takeaway
The Ehlers council complied with state housing law under duress while rhetorically opposing it. They approved Quail Meadows (448 units) after HCD threatened decertification, pursued the Our Neighborhood Voices initiative (which failed to qualify), and lobbied Sacramento fruitlessly. The city's 6th-cycle progress report shows strong above-moderate production (295% of allocation) but lagging affordable housing (29% of very-low, 21% of low-income).

### Cited Findings

- As of March 2026: 137 very-low units permitted (29% of allocation), 137 low-income (21%), 147 moderate (48%), and 1,206 above-moderate (295%). Twelve of 15 R-30 overlay sites are in process. — [Encinitas Action](https://encinitasaction.org/encinitas-housing-timeline-how-we-got-here-and-what-residents-should-know/)
- In February 2025, the council approved the 448-unit Quail Meadows project (90 affordable) after HCD warned that rejection would violate state law, could trigger Attorney General action, and could lead to Housing Element decertification and Builder's Remedy exposure. — [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2025/12/29/north-county-cities-are-conforming-to-state-housing-laws-but-working-to-change-them-behind-the-scenes/)
- The Encinitas Action timeline claims the current council has approved every housing development that has come before it, despite the anti-mandate rhetoric. — [Encinitas Action](https://encinitasaction.org/encinitas-housing-timeline-how-we-got-here-and-what-residents-should-know/)
- In 2020, a previous council adopted an ordinance to exempt the city from the state density bonus law. HCD issued a Notice of Violation in March 2021 demanding repeal. — [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2025/12/29/north-county-cities-are-conforming-to-state-housing-laws-but-working-to-change-them-behind-the-scenes/)

## What was the "Neighborhood Voices" effort?

### Takeaway
Our Neighborhood Voices is a statewide ballot initiative to amend the California constitution to give local governments the power to override state housing and land-use laws. Encinitas endorsed it under Ehlers, but it failed to qualify for the 2022, 2024, or 2026 ballots. The effort appears to be targeting 2028.

### Cited Findings
- The initiative would amend the California constitution so local land-use and zoning laws can override conflicting state laws. — [Planetizen](https://www.planetizen.com/blogs/116210-ballot-initiative-could-overturn-californias-zoning-reforms)
- The Encinitas council voted 4-1 to endorse the initiative. Oceanside, San Marcos, and Del Mar also endorsed it. Carlsbad did not. — [NBC San Diego](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/local/encinitas-joins-neighborhood-voices-effort-to-overrule-state-development-rule/3830034/); [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2025/12/29/north-county-cities-are-conforming-to-state-housing-laws-but-working-to-change-them-behind-the-scenes/)
- The initiative's first attempt (targeting 2022) failed partly due to COVID-19 impacts on signature gathering. A second attempt targeting 2024 also failed to qualify. A third attempt targeted 2026. — [Montecito Journal](https://www.montecitojournal.net/?p=38385); [Crescent Valley Weekly](https://www.crescentavalleyweekly.com/viewpoints/10/02/2025/op-ed-2/)
- The June 25, 2026 deadline for qualifying for the November 2026 ballot has passed with no indication the measure qualified. A Manifold prediction market on the question resolved "No." — [Manifold Markets](https://manifold.markets/DavidFWatson/will-our-neighborhood-voices-qualif)
- Ehlers said the initiative didn't previously qualify because housing projects were not yet visibly "on the ground," and believed that would change. — [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2025/03/07/encinitas-is-banking-on-the-state-to-let-them-limit-housing-but-lawmakers-arent-interested/)
- State lawmakers dismissed the effort. Sen. Blakespear said there is no appetite in Sacramento, and Assemblymember Chris Ward said courts would uphold state law. — [Voice of San Diego](https://voiceofsandiego.org/2025/03/07/encinitas-is-banking-on-the-state-to-let-them-limit-housing-but-lawmakers-arent-interested/)

### Inferences
- The initiative's repeated failure to qualify, combined with Sacramento's dismissal, means the Ehlers council's primary strategic vehicle for changing state housing law has gone nowhere. This may have contributed to voter fatigue with the "fight the state" message.
- The constitutional amendment approach was always a long shot. Even if it qualified, it would face well-funded opposition from the building industry and state housing advocates.

### Gaps
- Official Secretary of State records confirming that the initiative did not qualify for November 2026 were not directly verified. The conclusion is based on the passed deadline and absence of qualification reporting.

- The bikelash narrative's political utility may have peaked: the Santa Fe Drive implementation failures were real, but Blakespear's state intervention turned a local quality-of-life issue into a state-vs-city culture war that may have mobilized as many opponents as supporters.
