# 2026 Encinitas Election: Endorsements, Factions, and Political Signals

## Race Results (Unofficial, as of Friday Nov. 7 evening drop)

### Takeaway

Three races are being decided: Mayor (Viskanta +67 over Ehlers), District 3 (Golden +109 over Sprague), and District 4 (Lefkowits +856 over San Antonio). The mayor's race is razor-thin and has flipped multiple times. D4 is effectively called. D3 is narrow but trending toward Golden. About 390,000 ballots remain countywide; it is unclear how many are Encinitas-specific.

**Note on user-provided margins:** The prompt listed "Golden +109 (mayor), Lefkowits +856 (D3), White +1,062 (D4)." ## The Bike Lane / "Bikelash" Question

### Takeaway

The Santa Fe Drive protected bike lane removal was the most visible policy dispute in Encinitas heading into the election, but it did not map cleanly onto the candidate slates. Ehlers led the removal push, but Viskanta also took a nuanced position supporting modifications. Blakespear's state legislation to block the removal injected the issue into the campaign but also divided local advocates.

### Cited Findings

- Ehlers led the push to remove the protected bike lane in front of San Dieguito Academy, citing emergency response times, parking, and design flaws. -- [KPBS mayoral guide](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- The council voted to remove the physical bikeway protection and restore painted bike lanes and car parking. Joy Lyndes was the lone vote against. The redo was estimated at $3.5 million. -- [Streetsblog SD](https://sd.streetsblog.org/2026/09/08/california-legislature-moves-to-stop-encinitas-from-tearing-out-state-funded-santa-fe-bike-lanes/)
- Viskanta "backed changes to the Santa Fe Drive bike lane that city staff recommended, which the council did not adopt. She opposed a state bill provision, backed by State Sen. Catherine Blakespear, that bars the city from modifying the lane." -- [KPBS mayoral guide](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- Blakespear introduced SB 569 (statewide bike infrastructure protection), which died. She then secured budget trailer bill language specific to Santa Fe Drive, barring removal until 2030. -- [Streetsblog SD](https://sd.streetsblog.org/2026/09/08/california-legislature-moves-to-stop-encinitas-from-tearing-out-state-funded-santa-fe-bike-lanes/); [KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/09/10/the-encinitas-city-council-wants-to-remove-a-protected-bike-lane-a-state-senator-is-trying-to-save-it)
- The City Council considered a letter asking Gov. Newsom for a line-item veto of the Santa Fe provision. -- [Streetsblog SD](https://sd.streetsblog.org/2026/09/08/california-legislature-moves-to-stop-encinitas-from-tearing-out-state-funded-santa-fe-bike-lanes/)
- SD Bike Coalition posted a letter supporting a Coastal Act challenge to the removal. -- [SD Bike Coalition](https://sdbikecoalition.org/blog/mwl2rljnc2qvn6xph6h14a6fp1jeh6)

### Inferences

- The "bikelash" framing is an oversimplification. Unlike the 2024 cycle where Ehlers could run as a pure anti-bike-lane candidate against Kranz (who had championed the lane), in 2026 even Viskanta -- the Blakespear-backed challenger -- opposed the state legislation preventing the city from modifying the lane. The divide was over method (litigation and removal vs. redesign) rather than a binary pro-bike/anti-bike split.
- Blakespear's state-level intervention likely hurt more than helped locally. It reinforced the "state overreach" narrative that Ehlers and the anti-density faction have successfully weaponized since Prop A in 2013. Viskanta's decision to distance herself from Blakespear on this specific issue was a strategic necessity.
- The bike lane issue is a proxy for the deeper local control vs. state mandate tension. The question is not really about bike lanes per se; it is about whether Sacramento or the city council decides what happens on Encinitas streets.

### Gaps

- Whether SB 569 / the trailer bill language was signed by Newsom or vetoed could not be confirmed.
- Positions of Golden, Lefkowits, Sprague, and San Antonio on the bike lane were not found.

## Does This Repudiate the 2022 "Bikelash"?

### Takeaway

A Viskanta win + Golden win + Lefkowits win would represent a partial correction, not a full repudiation of the 2022-2024 anti-density/anti-bike backlash. The Democratic-endorsed candidates avoided running on explicitly pro-density or pro-bike platforms. Their wins would signal that voters want a middle path: neither the Blakespear-era aggressive state-aligned approach nor the Ehlers-era confrontational anti-everything posture.

### Cited Findings

- **2022 context:** Kranz won the mayoralty with ~47% in a multi-candidate field. Ehlers won D4. Joy Lyndes won D3. The council remained Democratic-leaning. -- [Patch 2022 roundup](https://patch.com/california/san-diego/north-county-election-roundup-where-races-stand-now)
- **2024 shift:** Ehlers beat Kranz for mayor (52-48). Luke Shaffer and Jim O'Hara won, creating a council with "one Democrat, one Republican appointee, and three independents." -- [iNewsource](https://inewsource.org/2026/10/07/encinitas-california-city-council-mayor-2026-election/); [California Globe](https://californiaglobe.com/fr/from-the-cheap-seats-judgment-in-encinitas/)
- **2026 challenger positioning:** Viskanta's housing platform emphasizes inclusionary zoning, mobile home park protections, and nonprofit-developer partnerships rather than increased density. She opposes the State Density Bonus Law's affordability threshold. She explicitly opposed Blakespear's state bike lane legislation. -- [KPBS mayoral guide](https://www.kpbs.org/news/politics/2026/10/12/meet-the-candidates-running-for-encinitas-mayor-2026-general-election)
- **Kranz assessment:** Tony Kranz said Ehlers' promise to "stop development" was the main reason he lost in 2024. -- [iNewsource](https://inewsource.org/2026/10/07/encinitas-california-city-council-mayor-2026-election/)
- **Voter registration:** Encinitas has roughly 20,000 registered Democrats, 11,000 Republicans, and 10,500 no-party-preference voters. -- [iNewsource](https://inewsource.org/2026/10/07/encinitas-california-city-council-mayor-2026-election/)

### Inferences

- The 2024 election was the peak of the anti-density backlash: voters replaced a Blakespear-aligned mayor with the author of Prop A. But Ehlers' council majority proved polarizing (the "tone" criticism aimed at O'Hara, the bike lane fight, contentious meetings), and 2026 represents the correction.
- Crucially, the Democratic candidates who are winning in 2026 are NOT running on the Blakespear/Kranz pro-density playbook. Viskanta explicitly distanced herself from state mandates on both housing and bike lanes. This is not a YIMBY revival; it is a "competent moderate" correction against what voters perceived as council dysfunction.
- The 20K-11K-10.5K registration split means Democrats can win Encinitas races if they nominate candidates who lean moderate on housing. Kranz lost because he was too closely identified with Blakespear's state-aligned approach. Viskanta is threading that needle.
- If all three Democratic-endorsed candidates win, the council composition shifts from 1D/1R/3I (with a conservative lean) to at least 3 Democratic-endorsed members (Lyndes is retiring, but Golden and Lefkowits are Dem-endorsed; Viskanta is a registered Democrat). The 2027 housing element planning cycle becomes the real test of whether this new majority is more accommodating to density.

- Prediction: Golden (D3) holds and grows slightly. Lefkowits (D4) is called. The mayor's race will be determined in the final 1-2 weeks of counting and could go either way.

## Summary: What These Results Signal About North County Politics

The 2026 Encinitas election, if the current leaders hold, signals five things about coastal North County politics:

1. **The anti-density backlash has a ceiling.** Ehlers won 52-48 in 2024 as a pure anti-development candidate. Two years later, with a governing record to defend, he is losing (barely). Voters punished the Blakespear/Kranz approach in 2024 but are now punishing the Ehlers/O'Hara approach. The sustainable political position in Encinitas is probably somewhere between these poles.

2. **Democrats can win in Encinitas by running to the center on housing.** Viskanta, Golden, and Lefkowits all received Democratic Party endorsements without running on YIMBY platforms. Viskanta's housing positions (inclusionary zoning over density bonus, mobile home park protections, nonprofit developers) are center-left, not supply-side. The YIMBY movement has no footprint in these races.

3. **Blakespear's network is the organizing force, but her policy brand is a liability.** Her endorsements, donor connections, and the county party infrastructure she helped build are carrying the Democratic slate. But her state-level bike lane legislation and housing mandate advocacy forced her own candidates to distance themselves on specific issues.

4. **DeMaio's Reform California is the organizing force on the right, not the Republican Party.** The official GOP endorsed in zero Encinitas races. Reform California endorsed in two. This pattern holds across North County.

5. **The 2027 housing element cycle is the real prize.** If Democratic-endorsed candidates take all three seats, the council majority for the next housing element planning process will be fundamentally different. Whether they actually approve more housing, or whether "working with the state" means accepting mandates grudgingly while pursuing the 2028 local control ballot measure, remains to be seen.
