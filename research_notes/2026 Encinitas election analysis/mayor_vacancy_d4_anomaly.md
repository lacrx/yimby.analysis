# Encinitas 2026 Election: Mayor's "Short Term" and District 4 Vote Anomaly

## Why is there a "short term" mayor's race in 2026?

### Takeaway
There is no vacancy. The Encinitas mayor has a **two-year term** by design, established when voters approved direct mayoral elections in 2014 but rejected Proposition M (which would have set a four-year term). Every Encinitas mayoral election is for a two-year term; the "short term" label simply reflects that the mayor's term is half the length of a council member's four-year term.

### Cited Findings
- Encinitas voters approved direct election of the mayor starting with the November 4, 2014 general municipal election. Proposition M, which proposed a four-year mayoral term, was "soundly defeated." -- [Encinitas Municipal Code / North Coast Current](https://www.northcoastcurrent.com/?p=1619)
- The city code states: "The Mayor is elected and shall serve a term of two years." Council members serve staggered four-year terms. -- [Encinitas Municipal Elections page](https://encinitasca.gov/Government/Municipal-Elections)
- "The Encinitas mayor serves a two-year term." -- [Fear and Loathing in Encinitas (Substack)](https://fearandloathinginencinitas.substack.com/p/encinitas-election-2026)
- Bruce Ehlers was elected mayor in November 2024, defeating incumbent Tony Kranz 52-48%. His term expires November 2026, which is the standard cycle. -- [NBC San Diego](https://nbcsandiego.com/news/local/san-diego-countys-mayoral-races-oceanside-within-300-votes-encinitas-incumbent-concedes/3674420); [Encinitas Municipal Elections](https://encinitasca.gov/Government/Municipal-Elections)

### Inferences
- The user's question assumed a vacancy caused the "short term" race. In reality, every Encinitas mayoral race is for a two-year term. No resignation, recall, death, or removal occurred.
- The 2026 race is a standard re-election cycle: Ehlers (incumbent) vs. Rimga Viskanta (challenger, SDUHSD trustee).

### Gaps
- None. The two-year term structure is well-documented across multiple sources.

## Did Bruce Ehlers resign, get recalled, die, or otherwise leave office?

### Takeaway
No. Ehlers won the 2024 mayoral election and is serving a normal two-year term. He is running for re-election in 2026. There was no vacancy.

### Cited Findings
- Ehlers defeated incumbent Mayor Tony Kranz in November 2024, 52% to 48%. Kranz conceded on November 8, 2024. -- [NBC San Diego](https://nbcsandiego.com/news/local/san-diego-countys-mayoral-races-oceanside-within-300-votes-encinitas-incumbent-concedes/3674420)
- The 2024 mayoral race was for "One (1) Mayor for a full two-year term with a term ending November 2026." -- [Encinitas Municipal Elections](https://encinitasca.gov/Government/Municipal-Elections)
- Ehlers is identified as the incumbent mayor in all 2026 election coverage. -- [North Coast Current](https://www.northcoastcurrent.com/election-2026/2026/11/encinitas-mayors-race-tightest-such-contest-in-county/); [Coast News](https://thecoastnews.com/election-2026-razor-thin-margins-in-encinitas-san-marcos/)

### Inferences
- The premise of the question (that Ehlers was "elected 2022") appears incorrect. Ehlers was elected mayor in 2024. In 2022, Tony Kranz was elected mayor for a two-year term (November 2022 to November 2024).
- Ehlers previously held the District 4 council seat. When he became mayor in late 2024, that seat became vacant.

### Gaps
- None. No vacancy occurred.

## Was there an interim/appointed mayor?

### Takeaway
No interim mayor was needed for the mayoral seat. However, Ehlers' move from District 4 to mayor created a **council vacancy** in District 4, which was filled by appointment.

### Cited Findings
- Marco San Antonio was appointed to the District 4 council seat on January 22, 2024, to fill the vacancy created by Ehlers' election as mayor. -- [Encinitas Municipal Elections](https://encinitasca.gov/Government/Municipal-Elections); [Fear and Loathing in Encinitas](https://fearandloathinginencinitas.substack.com/p/encinitas-election-2026)
- Similarly, when Tony Kranz moved from the District 1 council seat to become mayor (prior cycle), Allison Blackwell was appointed to fill his District 1 seat on January 18, 2023. -- [Encinitas Municipal Elections](https://encinitasca.gov/Government/Municipal-Elections)

### Inferences
- Encinitas has a pattern of council-to-mayor transitions creating downstream council vacancies that are filled by appointment. Both recent mayoral winners (Kranz in 2022, Ehlers in 2024) were sitting council members.

### Gaps
- None.

## What is the "short term" duration?

### Takeaway
The mayor always serves a two-year term. The 2026 winner will serve from late 2026 through November 2028.

### Cited Findings
- "For the upcoming election, the mayoral winner will serve a two-year term ending in November 2028. The District 3 and District 4 winners will receive four-year terms ending in November 2030." -- [Encinitas Municipal Elections](https://encinitasca.gov/Government/Municipal-Elections)

### Inferences
- The two-year cycle means Encinitas voters evaluate their mayor every even-year election, providing more frequent accountability than the four-year council terms.

### Gaps
- None.

## Why does "District 4" have ~16,000+ total votes while the mayor's race has ~5,200?

### Takeaway
**The ~16,000-vote race is NOT Encinitas District 4.** It is the **Escondido mayor's race** (Dane White vs. Elias Velazquez). The user appears to have mixed up two different cities' results, likely from a combined results table (such as Times of San Diego or the SD County Registrar).

### Cited Findings
- **Escondido mayor (citywide):** Dane White: 16,944 votes (~51.6%); Elias Velazquez: 15,882 votes (~48.4%). Total: ~32,826 votes. -- [Times of San Diego, Nov. 3, 2026](https://timesofsandiego.com/community/2026/11/03/election-results-nov-3-2026-general-election/)
- **Actual Encinitas District 4:** Brad Lefkowits: 3,175 (57.24%); Marco San Antonio: 2,372 (42.76%). Total: ~5,547 votes. -- [North Coast Current](https://www.northcoastcurrent.com/election-2026/2026/11/encinitas-mayors-race-tightest-such-contest-in-county/)
- **Encinitas mayor (citywide):** Rimga Viskanta: 10,373 (50.18%); Bruce Ehlers: 10,300 (49.82%). Total: ~20,673 votes. -- [North Coast Current](https://www.northcoastcurrent.com/election-2026/2026/11/encinitas-mayors-race-tightest-such-contest-in-county/)
- **Encinitas District 3:** Arielle Golden: 2,561 (50.96%); Ed Sprague: 2,465 (49.04%). Total: ~5,026 votes. -- [North Coast Current](https://www.northcoastcurrent.com/election-2026/2026/11/encinitas-mayors-race-tightest-such-contest-in-county/)
- Escondido's mayor is elected citywide (at-large) in a city of approximately 151,000 residents with approximately 81,000 registered voters. -- [Ballot Book](https://ballot-book-794606795f0b.herokuapp.com/jurisdictions/667/elections); [Escondido city elections page](https://www.escondido.org/city-election-information.aspx)
- Encinitas has approximately 63,000 residents. -- [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encinitas,_California)

### Inferences
- The vote total disparity is entirely explained by the city size difference: Escondido (~151K population, ~81K registered voters) is roughly 2.4x larger than Encinitas (~63K population). A citywide mayoral race in Escondido would naturally produce 3-6x the votes of a single Encinitas council district.
- The Encinitas District 4 totals (~5,547) are consistent with the other Encinitas races: District 3 (~5,026) and about one-quarter of the citywide mayoral total (~20,673), as expected for one of four districts.
- **The Dane White vs. Elias Velazquez matchup has nothing to do with Encinitas.** It is the Escondido mayor's race. These likely appeared adjacent in a county-wide results table, causing the confusion.

### Gaps
- The exact source where the user saw "District 4" labeled next to the White/Velazquez numbers is unknown; it may have been a misread of a multi-city results table.
