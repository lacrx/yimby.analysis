```markdown
## Anticipatory Compliance — The Threat Does the Work

The case law above records what happens when cities litigate and lose. It understates enforcement's actual reach, because most compliance never reaches a courtroom. An HCD warning that a denial would jeopardize housing element certification is frequently sufficient on its own, including against councils elected specifically to resist state mandates.

**Worked case — Quail Meadows, Encinitas (February 2025).** A council seated on an explicit anti-mandate platform, holding a 4-1 majority, voted **3-1 to approve a 448-unit project with 90 affordable units** after HCD warned that rejection risked housing element decertification and Builder's Remedy exposure. The mayor characterized the vote as a "Sophie's choice." Over the same council's term, the project approval record was reportedly unbroken: every housing development that came before it was approved.

**Why this works.** The decertification → Builder's Remedy chain is uniquely credible as a threat because it is close to automatic and it removes discretion wholesale rather than penalizing one decision:

| Ordinary HAA exposure | Decertification exposure |
|----------------------|-------------------------|
| Per-project, after the fact | Citywide, prospective |
| City retains discretion over other projects | City loses ability to deny any project with ≥20% affordable |
| Penalty is money (insurable, budgetable, appealable) | Penalty is loss of control (not recoverable by spending) |
| Requires a plaintiff and years of litigation | Attaches on HCD determination; BR vests at preliminary application |

A council can absorb a fine and tell its base it fought. It cannot absorb open-ended Builder's Remedy exposure and retain any claim to local control. **The threat that works is the one that takes away discretion, not the one that costs money.**

**Operational implications for advocates:**

1. **Request the HCD correspondence.** Technical assistance letters and certification warnings are the leverage, and they are disclosable. See [cpra-compliance](../pra-strategy/cpra-compliance.md).
2. **Surface decertification exposure before the hearing, not after the denial.** Post-denial litigation recovers the project in years; a pre-hearing exposure memo can recover it in one meeting.
3. **Frame exposure as loss of discretion.** Staff reports that quantify only HAA dollar penalties understate the stakes in the one dimension councils actually weigh.
4. **Document the approval record of anti-mandate majorities.** A council that campaigned on stopping development and then approved every project is the most effective available rebuttal to the next such campaign. See [infrastructure-backlash-cycle](../political-landscape/infrastructure-backlash-cycle.md).
```
